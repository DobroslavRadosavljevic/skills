# Do not publish

Fix these before the reader sees the text. One em dash in a paragraph can stay. A stack of them cannot.

Banned words live in [slop-words.md](slop-words.md). The list moves as models change. Ban the habit even if the word is new. Kobak et al. saw a jump in style words in PubMed after ChatGPT; “delve” led. Wikipedia *Signs of AI writing* lists the same cluster. This is a copy problem, not a court of authorship.

## CMS talk

The visitor does not care how the page is stored.

Cut: this site lists, this directory, this catalog, we only catalog, not a separate page here, SKU, front matter, stamped field, this entry, this record, this listing exists to, we index, we aggregate, our database of.

Talk about the product.

## Research leftover

Cut: this catalog omits, we do not invent, ISO dump, example countries in this catalog, pages we used, help copy, treat as history, according to our scrape, sources we checked, as of our last pass, data we could verify, unconfirmed so we skipped.

If you do not know, leave the sentence out. Do not explain the hole.

## Agent voice

Cut: do not mix both methods, always include, never claim, remember to, make sure to mention, as instructed, for the model, for the agent, follow the schema.

Those belong in this skill. Not in the article.

## Same skeleton

Do not reuse one H2 set, one opener, and one closer across entries. The dead giveaway is every page titled “Who it fits,” “How you connect,” “What to open next.”

Also cut: first sentences with one noun swapped; the same three-benefit stack with synonyms; FAQ questions cloned with a new product name; meta descriptions that only change the entity; `pros`/`cons` like “easy to use” / “can be expensive.”

## Words

Zero decorative uses from [slop-words.md](slop-words.md). Do not sneak one through.

## Sentence shapes

| Shape | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It’s not X. It’s Y. / Not just X, but Y | “It’s not a proxy. It’s a partner.” | Say the second thing. Drop the drumroll. |
| Whether you’re A or B | “Whether you’re a beginner or a pro” | Name one reader. |
| From X to Y, we’ve got you covered | | Say what you cover. |
| Two clipped fragments | “Fast. Simple.” | Write a sentence. |
| Double metaphor, no next step | “Less a hammer, more a scalpel.” | Say what to do. |
| Self-applause | “And that matters.” “This is the part everyone misses.” | Delete. |
| Warm-up | “Here’s the thing.” | Start on the fact. |
| Forced triples | “Faster, cheaper, smarter.” on every list | Two items, or four, or one true item. |
| Round range instead of a number | “5 to 10 minutes” when you mean ~7 | The number, or omit. |
| Summary closer | “In conclusion” restating the lede | Stop on the last real point. |
| Two-clause hook | “Most people think X. The reality is Y.” | A specific claim. |
| Adjective pair filler | “fast and reliable” | Pick the true one. |
| False balance | “While X has merits, Y is better” with no rule | Give the rule. |
| Hedge pile | “It may be possible that you might want to consider” | One hedge, or none. |
| Takeaway on every paragraph | “And so, X is essential.” | End on the fact. |
| In order to | | “to” |
| Boasts | | “has” |

Do not open a paragraph with Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Importantly, That said, With that in mind, That being said, Ultimately, Moving forward.

Do not pack two em dashes into a short paragraph. Use a period, a colon, a comma, or parentheses. Do not strip every dash to game a detector.

## Rhythm

Do not make every sentence the same length. Do not start three paragraphs the same way. Do not bullet what two sentences would hold. Do not give every page the same three-item list.

Keep each sentence inside the Simple English limits in `SKILL.md`. A four-word line plus an 18-word fact is fine. A wall of 22-word clones is not.

Do not fake “natural” with slang the product would never use.

## Cheap persuasion

No “Hey friend!!” No emoji weather. No hype the body cannot keep. No keyword soup in the title. No “#1” you cannot source. No fake countdown. No “it depends” with no criteria.

## UI and docs

No `OK` or `Submit` as the only confirm. No `Click here`. No `invalid` / `illegal` / “you entered incorrectly.” No `Oops!` on errors, bills, or deletes. Do not call the product `easy` or say they `just` do X. Do not leave a blank panel, or say `No data` while a query still runs. Do not mix a tutorial, a how-to, a reference, and an essay on one page. Do not say “the button on the right” (layout moves).

## Legal

Stay exact. Still no CMS notes, research leftovers, agent instructions, or the phrase list above.

## Hard to read

Cut jargon the reader did not ask for. Cut stacked nouns. Cut a 40-word sentence. Cut two names for one thing.

If a 12-year-old who knows the product name still cannot say what to do next, rewrite.
