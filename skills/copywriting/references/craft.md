# Draft

Facts first. Then headings. Then sentences. Then the sweep.

## Reader

Write to one person in one moment. A buyer picking a vendor. Someone staring at an empty inbox. Not “users.”

Use their words. Reviews, support tickets, the labels already on screen. Not the internal feature name.

Put the product name in the H1, early, or when the sentence is unclear without it. Not in every line.

## Outline

Write down what is true and different. Headings come from that list.

If you can paste a sibling’s name into the H2s and they still work, the H2s are lazy.

If the first 40 words work for another product, rewrite them.

If the close is “ready to get started” again, close on *this* choice instead.

`Pricing` and `FAQ` can stay in the nav. They cannot be the same three body sections on every page.

## Facts worth putting in

Price. Trial. What is gated. Quotas. Pool size. Retention. Sticky time. SLA. HQ or support hours if that changes the buy. Who should skip.

A real number beats “5 to 10.” If the decision is one sentence, write the sentence. A table can wait.

No fact? Skip the beat. Empty is better than padding.

## Promise

Title, H1, first 40 words, meta description, and the main button must agree. One job.

## Sentences

Active. Name the actor. Everyday words.

Instruction: 20 words or less. Description: 25 words or less. One idea per sentence.

On marketing and compare pages, keep paragraphs to a few sentences. Mix a short line with a longer fact. Stay inside the limits.

The first line under an H2 should pay the heading. Do not stall.

“You” for the reader. “We” for the company. Product named.

Docs and UI: ASD-STE100. Same word for the same idea. Define a hard term once.

Do not add slang, typos, or a fake childhood story so it “sounds human.”

## Fields the visitor sees

`seoTitle` / `seoDescription`: a pitch, not a keyword dump.

`shortDescription` / `summary` / `description`: what they get.

`verdict`: a call, plus a limit.

`pros` / `cons`: specific. Not “easy to use.”

`faq`: questions this reader has. Change them from page to page.

Match words already on the product. Do not stuff extra keywords into the H1.

## Many files

Write one file from facts. After a few, compare H2s and first lines. Fix clones before the next file. Going fast is not a reason to clone.
